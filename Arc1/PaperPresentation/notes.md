# Notes from the research paper

Argues that automation, thus also AI, is ill suited for wicked problems. 
Therefore we must use End-User Design (EUD) to mitigate this.

## Wicked problem

A wicked problem is a problem that is difficult to resolve due to it's poorly understood nature, ever-changing requirements or a structural difficulty (like requiring tons of people working simultaniously).

The article sketches 4 different characteristics:

- Problem being wicked, poorly understood or ill-defined. Requires the problems to be resolved in cycles, where solutions are assesed by owners, and refined over time.
- Problems where the world constantly changes, or the solution changes the problem space (road network is given as example).
- Problems that are very large, that indiviuals cannot solve.
- Problems that are very large, that require many different expertises.

The paper describes the above as problems that the UN defines as "problems societies are facing today", but later says that these are properties of wicked problems.
The former seems the most likely, as the wicked problem is described by the first point.

## End user design.

End use development is the notion of the end user designing and developing the system that they are using to fit their evolving needs and desires. The users need not necessarely be designers, programmers or any other type of specialist. A system must constantly adapt to new contexts, requirements and conditions, and as such systems must continuously evolve.  

The paper argues that "foudation models" (probably means AI models) are good at generating code, they are not good at discerning user needs and analysing systems in context. Thus the users of the system must provide the model their needs, after which the model will modify the system to conform with their changing requirements.
